Try dive sidemount

Interested in sidemount or double tanks but not sure it’s for you? A try dive lets you test the equipment before you buy it. You get to know the configuration in detail, it is adjusted to fit you, and on land you practise safety techniques such as the S-drill and V-drill. After the dive you talk through how it felt and get an assessment of your skills.

Duration: at least half a day. Equipment is included in the price.

SDI Sidemount Diver

Your entry into sidemount diving. You learn to handle sidemount equipment safely, manage gas with two independent cylinders, share gas and run the sidemount-specific S-drill. Christof advises you on equipment and fits it to you.

Format: at least 2 open water dives, run by Christof over 2 days. No decompression, maximum 40 m or the limit of your certification.

Prerequisites: SDI Open Water Scuba Diver or equivalent, minimum age 18 (15 with parental consent).

TDI Intro to Tech

The Intro to Tech Diving course from TDI offers the ideal introduction to technical diving for trained divers. Here you will learn the basic skills of dive planning, buoyancy, trim, balance, finning, gas planning, and emergency procedures. Your instructor will help you optimize your equipment settings for both backmount and sidemount diving. Christof runs the course over 3 days. The standard requires at least 3 dives, no deeper than 23 m, and at least 6 hours of classroom and briefings.

Prerequisites: SDI Open Water Scuba Diver or equivalent, 25 logged open water dives, minimum age 18 (15 with parental consent).

TDI Sidemount Diver

The TDI course is for technical divers who want to add sidemount to their tool kit. Compared with the SDI course it adds advanced skills and drills such as different finning techniques. Carrying additional cylinders is optional. Theory covers problem solving and diving through restrictions, among other topics.

Format: at least 2 days and 3 open water dives with a total bottom time of at least 90 minutes. 4 hours of classroom and briefings are suggested.

Prerequisites: SDI Open Water Scuba Diver or equivalent, minimum age 18.

TDI Advanced Nitrox

The Advanced Nitrox course from TDI teaches you everything you need to know to safely use Enriched Air up to 100% oxygen. It is aimed at divers who want to plan bottom times and decompression with oxygen-rich mixes. It is the first step towards decompression diving.

What you will learn: gas management, dive planning, equipment configuration, and skills, especially ascents and problem solving while maintaining your position within the team.

Format: 4 dives with a total bottom time of at least 100 minutes, at least 6 hours of classroom. Can be combined with Intro to Tech or TDI Sidemount.

Prerequisites: TDI Nitrox Diver or equivalent, 25 logged open water dives, minimum age 18 (15 with parental consent). Intro to Tech is recommended but not required.

TDI Decompression Procedures

This course covers the theory, planning and execution of planned staged decompression dives to a maximum of 45 metres. Decompression is done on nitrox or oxygen, as far as the mix is covered by your certification, which is why the course is often combined with Advanced Nitrox.

What you practise: gas planning based on your own and your team’s consumption, an exact decompression profile, carrying, dropping and retrieving the stage cylinder, gas switches as a team, deploying an SMB solo and as a team, controlled ascents on an SMB or line, deploying a backup mask and responding to a gas loss.

Scope: 4 dives, 2 of them deeper than 30 metres, and at least 6 hours of theory and briefings. Combined with Advanced Nitrox it is 6 dives; combined with Intro to Tech or TDI Sidemount plus Advanced Nitrox at least 8. In open water at most four students per instructor.

Prerequisites: SDI Advanced Adventure Diver, Advanced Diver or equivalent, 25 logged open water dives, minimum age 18.

GUE Open Water Diver

GUE Open Water Diver is the entry course for non-divers. From the first session you learn what every GUE course builds on: stable buoyancy, horizontal trim, efficient propulsion and diving as a team. Nitrox and gas analysis are part of the course.

The course normally runs over five days. It includes at least ten confined water sessions, six open water dives and at least 40 hours of instruction, made up of theory, land drills and in-water training. Every skill is introduced in shallow, calm water first, and Christof uses the lake for this rather than a swimming pool.

Maximum depth during the course is 21 metres, and all dives stay within no-decompression limits. In the water there are at most three students per instructor, and the course can also run with a single student.

Prerequisites: minimum age 12, with parental consent for minors. Divers under 15 may only dive under the direct supervision of an adult holding at least an autonomous diver certification.

GUE Advanced Open Water Diver

GUE Advanced Open Water Diver prepares you for deeper recreational diving. The course covers decompression theory and correct ascents, rescue on land, at the surface and underwater, emergency management, and navigation with compass, guideline and natural features. Nitrox extends bottom time; triox, if you choose it, reduces narcosis and gas density.

The course normally runs over six days with at least eleven dives, including one in reduced ambient light, and at least 48 hours of instruction. Maximum depth is 30 metres, and all dives stay within no-decompression limits. Triox is noted on the certification if at least two dives are made with it.

You can also earn the certification by completing the Navigation, Rescue and Deep Primers individually.

Prerequisites: GUE Open Water Diver, Performance Diver or Fundamentals, followed by at least 15 non-training dives, minimum age 16, and basic life support (BLS) training within the last 24 months. During the course: swim 275 metres in under 14 minutes and 15 metres underwater on a breath hold.

GUE Fundamentals

The Fundamentals allows divers of all levels to improve their skills and prepares them for their future diving life and, if applicable, a safe introduction to technical diving. Since 2025 a new course structure divides the Fundamentals course into three courses.


Performance Diver

You have completed your initial scuba training (OWD or similar) and would like to refine and consolidate your diving skills. Then the Performance Diver course is for you. It is aimed at all divers, regardless of whether they want to pursue technical diving or not. This two-day course (4 dives) will help you refine your basic skills (buoyancy, trim, balance, fin kicks, teamwork). You will also learn how to dive with Nitrox.

More information


Basic Fundamentals

The Basic Fundamentals course is your introduction to further diving courses with GUE (primer courses). It allows non-GUE divers to learn about the configuration and mindset of GUE and to develop a solid foundation of skills. No matter where you are in your diving, the Fundamentals course will help you become a better diver. The course can be completed with either single or double tanks and optionally in a dry suit (experience required).

More information


Technical Fundamentals

The Technical Fundamentals course is aimed at experienced divers who are interested in advanced courses in technical, rebreather, or cave diving. Prerequisites include certification to 30 meters, 75 non-training dives and 25 dives with doubles.

GUE DPV 1

Every diver knows the feeling of wanting to dive a little further. A DPV allows you to reach different dive sites or spend more time at your dive destination, e.g. a wreck. In this 3-day course (min. 5 dives), you will learn how to use a DPV safely, how to plan your gas consumption, and you will practice all skills and emergency scenarios with a DPV. Prerequisites: GUE Open Water Diver, Performance Diver or Fundamentals, at least 25 non-training dives since, at least 75 dives in total, minimum age 16.

GUE Navigation Primer

Autonomous divers should be able to find their way underwater. In this course, you will learn about natural navigation, as well as using a compass and a map. You will also learn how to work with lines, and at the end of the course, you will be able to create a map on your own. The core of Christof Müller’s course is learning how to plan and execute a dive in a reproducible manner. This will allow you to truly explore the underwater world. The Navigation Primer is a two-day course with at least three dives, including one dive in reduced ambient light conditions, for example a night dive. Prerequisites: GUE Open Water Diver, Performance Diver or Fundamentals, plus at least 15 dives since.

Christof Müller

Christof is a seasoned trainer, both in his main job as an IT consultant and trainer with over 25 years of experience and as a diving instructor. Here his passion and focus is to prepare his students for the challenges of technical diving. He does so both within GUE as well as in SDI / TDI when it comes to sidemount diving. He trains both newcomers and experienced technical divers.

He himself has logged thousands of dives in cold water here in Germany (lakes & mines), as well as in warmer waters (Malta, Gozo, Croatia) and especially in the cenotes of Mexico, where he completed his cave training up to CCR full cave. The complete list of certifications as a diver can be found below. He has logged over 200 hours on the Kiss Sidewinder (CCR) since 2021 and also dives both in sidemount and backmount doubles in open circuit. He completed his own technical training with GUE, TDI, ISE, and PSAI.


As a diving instructor, he will guide you through GUE’s Fundamentals, DPV1, or Navigation Primer courses, as well as SDI and TDI sidemount courses, Intro to Tech and Advanced Nitrox. You can find the complete list and further information under “Courses.”

Several days a month, you can find Christof at Deepstop in Schwetzingen. He is happy to advise you with regards to equipment and training and is the shop’s specialist for all things sidemount. As such, he is happy to adjust your new sidemount system for you. Deepstop also serves as his base for all courses. The proximity to various lakes further simplifies organization and ensures that everything runs smoothly.

Christof loves and lives technical diving and can show you many valuable tips and tricks. His philosophy as an instructor is to train thinking divers. This means you will not only acquire a solid foundation of skills that you can build on in your technical diving courses, but also develop a genuine understanding of the theory, which will enable you to make your own decisions. His own diving goes up to full cave and rebreather, and that experience feeds into his teaching.

How training works

Open water, not pool

Dives take place in local lakes such as Rheinauer See or St. Leon. Courses normally run from April to November.

Your gear gets fitted

Before the first dive your equipment is checked and adjusted to you. Missing items can be rented.

Land, water, debrief

Every course day: theory at Deepstop, dry runs on land, 1 to 2 dives, then a detailed debrief.

Personal training

No new course needed: based on video analysis, Christof works with you on specific skills.

More about personal training

Personal training

It doesn’t always have to be a new course. Sometimes it’s just helpful to work on specific skills with an experienced trainer. Based on a video analysis of your specific problems and deficits, Christof will help you improve your diving skills and achieve your personal goals.
