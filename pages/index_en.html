SDI Sidemount Diver

Your entry into recreational sidemount diving. You learn to handle sidemount equipment safely, manage gas with two independent cylinders, share gas and run the sidemount-specific S-drill. I advise you on equipment and fit it to you.

Format: at least 2 open water dives, over 2 days with me. No decompression, maximum 40 m or the limit of your certification.

SDI or TDI: if you want to use sidemount on decompression dives later, TDI Sidemount Diver is the better fit.

Prerequisites: SDI Open Water Scuba Diver or equivalent, minimum age 18 (15 with parental consent).

TDI Intro to Tech

Intro to Tech is the step into technical diving for certified divers, strictly without decompression. You work on dive planning, buoyancy, trim and balance, finning, gas planning and emergency procedures in a technical configuration. I teach the course in sidemount and set up your kit with you.

Format: 3 days with me. The standard requires at least 3 dives, no deeper than 23 m, and at least 6 hours of classroom and briefings.

Combination: taught together with TDI Advanced Nitrox, the standard requires 4 dives for both courses. Decompression Procedures is the next step.

Prerequisites: SDI Open Water Scuba Diver or equivalent, 25 logged open water dives, minimum age 18 (15 with parental consent).

TDI Sidemount Diver

The TDI course is for divers who want to use sidemount in technical diving as well. After the course you may dive sidemount with safety and decompression stops, as far as your other certifications allow. Compared with the SDI course it adds advanced skills and drills such as different finning techniques. Carrying additional cylinders is optional. Theory covers problem solving and diving through restrictions, among other topics.

Format: at least 2 days and 3 open water dives with a total bottom time of at least 90 minutes. 4 hours of classroom and briefings are suggested. Can be combined with Advanced Nitrox (4 dives in total) and with Decompression Procedures.

Prerequisites: SDI Open Water Scuba Diver or equivalent, minimum age 18.

SDI DPV

On the SDI DPV course you learn to handle a scooter (DPV, diver propulsion vehicle) safely: handling, dive planning, procedures and techniques. We go through the typical problems and hazards of diving with a DPV and how to avoid them. I teach the course in sidemount as well.

Format: 2 days with me, at least 2 dives.

TDI Advanced Nitrox

On TDI Advanced Nitrox you learn to use mixes from 21 to 100 % oxygen safely. The course is for divers who want to plan bottom time and decompression with oxygen-rich mixes, and it is the first step towards decompression diving.

What you learn: gas management, dive planning, equipment configuration and skills, especially ascents and problem solving while keeping your position in the team.

Format: 4 dives with a total bottom time of at least 100 minutes, at least 6 hours of classroom. Combined with Intro to Tech or TDI Sidemount, 4 dives cover both courses.

Prerequisites: TDI Nitrox Diver or equivalent, 25 logged open water dives, minimum age 18 (15 with parental consent). I recommend Intro to Tech first, but it is not required.

TDI Decompression Procedures

This course covers the theory, planning and execution of planned staged decompression dives to a maximum of 45 metres. Decompression is done on nitrox or oxygen, as far as the mix is covered by your certification, which is why the course is often combined with Advanced Nitrox.

What you practise: gas planning based on your own and your team’s consumption, an exact decompression profile, carrying, dropping and retrieving the stage cylinder, gas switches as a team, deploying an SMB solo and as a team, controlled ascents on an SMB or line, deploying a backup mask and responding to a gas loss.

Scope: 4 dives, 2 of them deeper than 30 metres, and at least 6 hours of theory and briefings. Combined with Advanced Nitrox it is 6 dives; combined with Intro to Tech or TDI Sidemount plus Advanced Nitrox at least 8. At most three students per course.

Prerequisites: SDI Advanced Adventure Diver, Advanced Diver or equivalent, 25 logged open water dives, minimum age 18.

GUE Open Water Diver

GUE Open Water Diver is the entry course for non-divers. From the first session you learn what every GUE course builds on: stable buoyancy, horizontal trim, efficient propulsion and diving as a team. Nitrox and gas analysis are part of the course.

The course normally runs over five days. It includes at least ten confined water sessions, six open water dives and at least 40 hours of instruction, made up of theory, land drills and in-water training. Every skill is introduced in shallow, calm water first, and I use the lake for this rather than a swimming pool.

Maximum depth during the course is 21 metres, and all dives stay within no-decompression limits. In the water there are at most three students per instructor, and the course can also run with a single student.

Prerequisites: minimum age 12, with parental consent for minors. Divers under 15 may only dive under the direct supervision of an adult holding at least an autonomous diver certification.

GUE Advanced Open Water Diver

GUE Advanced Open Water Diver prepares you for deeper recreational diving. The course covers decompression theory and correct ascents, rescue on land, at the surface and underwater, emergency management, and navigation with compass, guideline and natural features. Nitrox extends bottom time; triox, if you choose it, reduces narcosis and gas density.

The course normally runs over six days with at least eleven dives, including one in reduced ambient light, and at least 48 hours of instruction. Maximum depth is 30 metres, and all dives stay within no-decompression limits. Triox is noted on the certification if at least two dives are made with it.

You can also earn the certification by completing the Navigation, Rescue and Deep Primers individually.

Prerequisites: GUE Open Water Diver, Performance Diver or Fundamentals, followed by at least 15 non-training dives, minimum age 16, and basic life support (BLS) training within the last 24 months. During the course: swim 275 metres in under 14 minutes and 15 metres underwater on a breath hold.

GUE Fundamentals

Since standards v10, the former Fundamentals has been split into three courses: Performance Diver, Basic Fundamentals and Technical Fundamentals. All three work on buoyancy, trim, balance, propulsion and team diving, each with a different goal.

Performance Diver: for divers with a first certification (OWD or similar) who want to consolidate their core skills, whether or not they plan to go technical. Two days, four dives. Diving with nitrox is part of it.

GUE Performance Diver

Basic Fundamentals: the way into further GUE training, such as the primers. You get to know GUE’s configuration and way of working and build a solid set of skills. The course runs in a single tank or doubles, and in a drysuit if you have experience in one.

GUE Basic Fundamentals

Technical Fundamentals: for experienced divers aiming for further courses in technical, rebreather or cave diving. Prerequisites include a certification to 30 metres, 75 non-training dives and 25 dives in doubles.

GUE Navigation Primer

Autonomous divers should be able to find their way underwater. On the Navigation Primer you learn natural navigation, compass and map navigation and line work, and by the end you draw a map of your own. With me, the focus is on planning and running a dive in a way you can repeat.

Format: 2 days, at least 3 dives, including one in reduced ambient light, for example a night dive.

Prerequisites: GUE Open Water Diver, Performance Diver or Fundamentals, plus at least 15 dives since.

Christof Müller

Christof has worked as an IT trainer and consultant for more than 25 years and has taught diving for many years. Underwater he prepares divers for the demands of technical diving, on the GUE path and in sidemount under SDI/TDI, from newcomers to experienced technical divers.

He has logged many thousands of dives in cold water in Germany (lakes and mines), in warmer waters (Malta, Gozo, Croatia) and above all in the cenotes of Mexico, where he did his cave training up to Full Cave and Cave CCR. He has more than 200 hours on the KISS Sidewinder since 2021 and dives sidemount (OC) and backmount doubles in his own time. His own technical training was with GUE, TDI, ISE and PSAI. That experience feeds into his teaching; he does not teach cave courses.

As an instructor he takes you through GUE courses from Open Water Diver via Fundamentals and primers to DPV 1, and through the sidemount courses under SDI and TDI up to Intro to Tech and Decompression Procedures. All courses are listed on the home page under sidemount and GUE.

Several days a month Christof is at Deepstop in Schwetzingen, where he advises customers in the shop, is the contact for sidemount systems and sets them up. Deepstop is also the base for his courses: theory in the training room, with the local lakes a short drive away.

How training works

Open water, not pool

Dives take place in local lakes such as Rheinauer See or St. Leon. Courses normally run from April to November.

Your gear gets fitted

Before the first dive I check your equipment and adjust it to you. Missing items can be rented.

Land, water, debrief

Every course day: theory at Deepstop, dry runs on land, 1 to 2 dives, then a detailed debrief.

Personal training

No new course needed: based on video analysis, I work with you on specific skills.

More about personal training

Personal training

Not every goal needs a new course. In personal training we work on specific skills. The basis is a video analysis of your dives: we look at where things go wrong, and I show you how to fix them. We agree on the content beforehand.

Price: half day €100, full day €225, plus gas and lake entry.
